# Demo video

Agreed 2026-10-09, updated 2026-11-07 for the changes since (drag-to-loop, the Mac and
Vim keymaps, the Light and Dark palettes, the empty states).

Record everything in one pass, in the order below. In the edit, cut the hook out of it
and put it at the top of the README, linking to the full video, which opens with the
same hook. Two minutes at most: quick, clean, no fluff.

## The hook

A loop repeating over the solo in the practice view, the stem lanes underneath, and one
key press muting the guitar lane while the rest plays on. In one picture: loop the hard
part, take the guitar out, play along. It comes from step 13. If it needs a second beat,
cut to the speed dropping with the waveform still looping; more than that and it stops
being a hook.

On GitHub a short video doesn't play by itself, it shows a play button. Export the hook
as a 4 to 6 second GIF or animated WebP (2 to 3 seconds is gone before anyone notices
it), above the two screenshots in the README.

## Recording order

### Library (about 25 s)

1. Empty riff: "Where is your music?", pick a music folder, the songs appear.
2. Scroll fast, then jump with the letter rail.
3. Play a song from the list.
4. Search for a song (with the Mac keys, just start typing).
5. Queue two songs, show them in the sidebar, remove one.
6. Add a few songs to a new playlist.
7. Search for the playlist by name and open it.
8. Search inside the playlist, then remove a song from it.

### Practice (about 80 s)

9. Tab into practice.
10. Drag across the waveform to set a loop over a phrase.
11. Turn the grid on and drag a loop edge so it snaps to a beat (drag it once with the
    grid off first, so the snap is visible).
12. Save the loop as a section.
13. Drop the stems folder on the song: the `+N` count appears, switch with `v`. Mute
    the guitar, solo the bass. **The hook is here.**
14. Speed down, then pitch up a semitone.
15. Start the trainer, the speed climbing each time round (sped up in the edit).
16. Hold Tab, so the views flick back and forth instantly.
17. ⌘Q, reopen from the Dock: the same song, loop, section, stems and speed, straight
    away.

Stems come before speed so slowing down and training happen with the stems, which looks
like real practice, and the hook comes before the slower trainer part.

## Before recording

- **Use a demo data folder** (settings, at the bottom), so step 1 happens on an empty
  riff and your own library stays private. A fresh data folder starts with the Mac keys
  and the T3 scheme on Auto; pick Light or Dark on purpose for the video.
- **A local music folder, not Drive**: a cloud-only first scan stalls on camera.
- **Show the keys** with a keystroke overlay such as KeyCastr. Viewers can't see what
  you press.
- **Sound matters**: speed, pitch, muting and soloing only land if viewers hear them.
- **Music rights**: a public video with commercial songs can get flagged or muted. Use
  songs you have the rights to, or a freely licensed multitrack (the Cambridge Music
  Technology multitracks come with stems; check their licence for a public demo).
- **The trainer is slow in real time**: a short loop with "+5% every repeat", or speed
  that part up in the edit.
- **Prepare the stems**: a folder ready to drop with nothing else in it. Lining up takes
  about a second.
- **Persistence**: quit with ⌘Q, the path that saves everything.
